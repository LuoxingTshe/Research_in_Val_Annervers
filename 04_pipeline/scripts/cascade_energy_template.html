Net output at η 0.85 comes to about 529 GWh against 453.9 GWh actually generated in 2025, a gap of about 76 GWh. Main causes: the Moiry–Mottec stage was shut for six months for the Tsarmette works, Moiry ended the year holding only 36.5 hm³ (part of the inflow carried into 2026), the Vissoie units were not all back in service, and flood peaks exceeded intake capacity and spilled.

How minimum ecological flows are kept

Residual flows are obligations at cross-sections, relayed downstream: water released upstream counts toward the next section, so 90, 300 and 470 L/s are not additive. Annual volumes below assume the minimum is held all year.

If all of this water could otherwise have been turbined, residual flows cost at most about 22.6 GWh of net output a year, roughly 5 % of 2025 generation. In the summer melt season intakes often run above capacity and spill, so the minimum is largely met by overflow; the real competition with generation is in winter. The ~130 kW ecological turbine inside Moiry dam generates with the release water before it returns to the Gougra, so that output and the residual flow are the same water.

Pump in summer, generate in winter

Monthly averages of 2025 Swiss day-ahead prices: winter (Oct–Mar) averaged about half again as much as summer (Apr–Sep). Moiry's job is to hold summer inflow until winter and then send it through all three stages.

Method

  • Reporting areas are mapped to where their water enters: Moiry and Tourtemagne into upper storage; the "Mottec" area (upper Navizence + Moulins) into the Mottec–Vissoie conveyance; the "Vissoie" area (incl. Fang) into the lower tunnel. This mapping is an assumption; the annual report does not publish turbine volumes per plant.
  • Tourtemagne water can be turbined directly at Mottec (613 m gross head) or siphon-pumped into Moiry (then 685 m). The split is not published; the table counts it as direct. Lona plant volume is estimated by catchment share (6.2 / 35.5 km²).
  • Residual flows are converted to annual volumes assuming year-round compliance and are taken entirely out of generating water (upper bound). The Gougra release is credited toward the Navizence 300 L/s reach; lateral inflow along the reach is ignored.
  • Irrigation at allocation ceilings: Roux + St-Luc free quota 152,000 m³; Briey 45 L/s and Niouc < 60 L/s over 30 Apr–15 Sep (139 days). Snowmaking at 0.11 % of catchment inflow. Ricard and Granges draw from the lower residual reach in unknown amounts and are not shown.
  • Diagram widths: hatched bands (snowmaking, irrigation, the extra release at the Mottec intake and the Vissoie-area share of the 470 L/s) are estimates or ceilings. They are drawn at a minimum of 4 hm³ so they stay readable; labels and tooltips give the modelled value.
  • Pumping energy: siphon pump head 0–126 m, taken as 63 m at 80 % efficiency, ≈ 0.21 kWh/m³; storage pump head 570–664 m, taken as 617 m at 85 %, ≈ 1.98 kWh/m³. Prices are monthly means; actual pumping is concentrated in low-price midday hours, so real gains are larger.
  • FMG is a partner company settled at cost plus 10 % (2025 profit 3.01 MCHF), so market gains and losses fall on its shareholders (Alpiq 54 %, Rhonewerke 27.5 %, municipalities and others). Water fees and special tax (8.70 MCHF) are levied on statutory theoretical power at 110 CHF/kWth and do not change with seasonal strategy.
